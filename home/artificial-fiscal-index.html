Artificial Fiscal Index: 2-Parameter Deflation

A Mathematical Investigation of a Proposed Nonlinear Deflation Function

Original mathematical formulation: Recast Theorist / SystemaDock
Mathematical analysis: GPT-6

Jump to the proposed price-deflation model

Abstract

This research note examines a two-variable nonlinear expression featuring nested quotients, a cube root, exponential terms, and a variable exponent. We investigate its behavior as a candidate mathematical attenuation index. The term “fiscal” is provisional: no relationship to observed price indices has yet been established.

Confirmed nested-division expression

\[f(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{x+y}}{2}\left[\frac{\left(\frac{y}{x}+\sqrt[3]{x^3+y^3}\right)\sqrt{xe^x+ye^y}}{xy(x+y)^6e^{xy}}\right]^{\frac{1}{x+y}}\]

For the analysis here, the domain is \(x>0,\ y>0\). The expression is strictly positive and differentiable on this domain.

Proposed price-deflation model

Let x be an input percentage expressed as a decimal (10% = 0.10), and y the original price. The original nonlinear function f(x,y) is interpreted as the calculated deflated price. The proposed difference and effective rate are:

\[D(x,y)=y-f(x,y)\] \[R(x,y)=100\left(1-\frac{f(x,y)}{y}\right)\]

A positive D means the calculated output is below the original price; a negative D indicates an increase. The model is nonlinear, so a 10% input need not produce a 10% reduction.

Functional structure

The nested quotient \(A/(B/C)=AC/B\) is crucial. This structure produces the sixth power \((x+y)^6\) in the denominator after simplification. Replacing the nested quotient with a product changes the function and invalidates numerical conclusions.

Attenuation and amplification

Attenuation in the first coordinate is characterized locally by \(\partial f/\partial x<0\); in the second, by \(\partial f/\partial y<0\). Simultaneous attenuation requires both inequalities. A decrease along \(x=y\) alone does not prove global monotonicity. The exponential term and the variable exponent make the behavior sensitive to input scale and direction.

Research status: This is an exploratory mathematical model, not a validated economic deflation indicator. The numerical table above was recomputed from the confirmed expression. Earlier inconsistent numerical tables and turning-point claims are not relied upon.

Reproducible evaluation

For numerical work, use the logarithmic form below to reduce overflow and underflow:

\[\log f=\frac12\log(x+y)-\log 2+\frac{\log\!\left(\frac yx+\sqrt[3]{x^3+y^3}\right)+\frac12\log(xe^x+ye^y)-\log x-\log y-6\log(x+y)-xy}{x+y}.\]

Dimensional and economic limitations

The unmodified formula adds a dimensionless percentage to a monetary price and exponentiates quantities involving the price. As a result, changing dollars to cents changes the function nontrivially. A unit-consistent price normalization and calibration against observed data are necessary before interpreting outputs as fiscal predictions. The zero-percent case x = 0 is also outside the stated domain.

Next steps

A complete research treatment should calculate the gradient and Hessian, map the attenuation region, prove relevant asymptotic results, and test whether any economic interpretation survives comparison with observed data.
