Artificial Fiscal Index
2-Parameter Deflation

An Investigation of Nonlinear Price Transformation, Functional Division, and Mathematical Attenuation

Original mathematical formulation: Recast Theorist / SystemaDock
Mathematical exposition and analysis: GPT-6 · November 2026

◇ ✦ ◇
Abstract. We examine a proposed two-parameter price transformation and its associated signed difference index. The author defines the index as the original price minus a nonlinear transformation, \(D(x,y)=y-I(x,y)\), where \(x\) denotes a percentage entered as a decimal and \(y\) denotes a price. The transformation contains nested quotients, radicals, and exponential functions. We preserve its agreed algebraic form, analyze its interpretation, and distinguish mathematical behavior from empirical economic validation. The index is exploratory: its dimensional dependence and undefined zero-percentage case require further study.

01The central construction

A conventional price adjustment applies a fixed fraction to a starting price. The proposed Artificial Fiscal Index instead treats the adjusted quantity as the output of a nonlinear transformation. The author's defining difference is:

\[\boxed{D(x,y)=y-I(x,y)}\]

Here \(x>0\) is a percentage written as a decimal (for example, \(0.10\) for ten percent), and \(y>0\) is the initial price. Crucially, there is no further division by \(y\) in the definition. If \(I(x,y)0\); if \(I(x,y)>y\), then \(D<0\). “Inflated” describes the proposed transformation, not a theorem that its value always exceeds \(y\).

02The original nonlinear transformation

The agreed nested-division transcription is:

\[I(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{x+y}}{2}\left[\frac{\displaystyle\frac{y}{x}+\sqrt[3]{x^3+y^3}}{\displaystyle\frac{(x+y)^3e^{xy}}{\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{xe^x+ye^y}}{xy(x+y)^3}}}\right]^{1/(x+y)}\]

For positive \(x,y\), the identity \(A/(B/C)=AC/B\) gives the equivalent expression:

\[\boxed{I(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{x+y}}{2}\left[\frac{\left(\frac{y}{x}+\sqrt[3]{x^3+y^3}\right)\sqrt{xe^x+ye^y}}{xy(x+y)^6e^{xy}}\right]^{1/(x+y)}}\]

The simplification changes presentation, not the defined function. In particular, the sixth power of \(x+y\) follows from the two cubic factors in the nested denominator. Substituting this expression into \(D(x,y)=y-I(x,y)\) yields the complete proposed index.

03Mathematical interpretation

On the positive quadrant, every factor inside the fractional power is positive, so \(I(x,y)>0\). Thus \(D(x,y)

For fixed \(y\), the percentage response is:

\[\frac{\partial D}{\partial x}=-\frac{\partial I}{\partial x}\]

For fixed \(x\), the price response is:

\[\frac{\partial D}{\partial y}=1-\frac{\partial I}{\partial y}\]

The sign of \(D\) and the direction of change in \(D\) are separate questions. A positive difference at one input does not prove that increasing \(x\) produces a larger price reduction.

05Economic and dimensional limitations

The expression combines \(x\), a dimensionless percentage, with \(y\), a monetary price, in sums and exponential arguments. Changing the unit of \(y\) from dollars to cents changes the numerical result in a way that does not preserve the same physical price relationship. The current model is therefore not unit invariant.

A future specification might introduce a reference price \(P_0\) and dimensionless input \(p=y/P_0\), but that would be a new model, not an implicit alteration of the author's expression. The original transformation is undefined at \(x=0\), so a zero-percent no-change baseline is not guaranteed.

06Conclusion

The defining contribution is the two-stage structure: transform a price with a nonlinear two-parameter function, then subtract that result from the original price. The signed difference has an immediate algebraic interpretation. Its mathematical interest lies in nested functional division and potentially complex response regions. Economic usefulness remains a hypothesis, contingent on unit-consistent specification, monotonicity and boundary testing, and empirical comparison.
