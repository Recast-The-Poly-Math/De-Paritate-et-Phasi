Generalized Grow
Quantified Deflation

A Four-Parameter Extension of the Artificial Fiscal Index

Original model: Recast Theorist · Mathematical exposition and critical analysis: GPT-6

Abstract

Generalized Grow Quantified Deflation (GGQD) is a nonlinear four-parameter construction built from the two-parameter Artificial Fiscal Index. This essay specifies the composition, checks a photographed numerical example, proves conditional sensitivity results, and distinguishes mathematical consistency from economic validity. GGQD remains an experimental proposal pending independently defined economic targets and empirical testing.

✦ ◇ ✦

I · Definition

The original AFI2 function is denoted by \(I(x,y)\). Its derived deflation residual is \(\mathrm{AFIX}(x,y)=y-I(x,y)\). The proposed universality-adjusted intermediate quantity is:

\[\mathrm{GG}(n,x,y,u)=\frac{\sqrt{n+u}\,[y-I(x,y)]}{\sqrt[3]{(1+x)^3+(1+y)^3}}\]

The final generalized quantity is:

\[\boxed{\mathrm{GGQD}(n,x,y,u)=\sqrt{n^2+y^2}-\frac{\sqrt{n+u}\,[y-I(x,y)]}{\sqrt[3]{(1+x)^3+(1+y)^3}}}\]

The precise original AFI2 expression is retained by reference rather than replaced by an unverified transcription. The index u needs an operational definition before empirical economic use.

II · Calculator verification

The author's photographed example reports \(I(2,100)=1.31310365217\). Thus \(\mathrm{AFIX}(2,100)=98.68689634783\). For \((n,x,y,u)=(55,2,100,3)\), the intermediate GG is approximately \(7.44129154418\), giving:

\[\mathrm{GGQD}(55,2,100,3)\approx106.685830561\]

This is a numerical reproduction, not a demonstration of forecasting accuracy.

III · Domain and structural properties

For nonnegative x and y, the cube-root denominator is positive. Real-valued evaluation requires \(n+u\ge 0\) and a defined \(I(x,y)\). For unrestricted real x and y, the denominator vanishes at \(x+y=-2\). Smoothness otherwise depends on the regularity of the original AFI2 function.

IV · Universality theorem

Write \(A(x,y)=[y-I(x,y)]/\sqrt[3]{(1+x)^3+(1+y)^3}\). Holding x and y fixed:

\[G(n,u)=\sqrt{n^2+y^2}-A\sqrt{n+u}\]
\[\frac{\partial G}{\partial u}=-\frac{A}{2\sqrt{n+u}},\qquad \frac{\partial^2G}{\partial u^2}=\frac{A}{4(n+u)^{3/2}}\]

Where A is positive, GGQD decreases and is convex in universality u. If A is negative, the inequalities reverse. The normal-price derivative is \(\partial G/\partial n=n/\sqrt{n^2+y^2}-A/(2\sqrt{n+u})\), whose sign need not be fixed.

V · Economic scrutiny

The most demanding questions concern units, price normalization, zero-inflation behavior, monotonicity, and nonnegative outputs. A currency-valued result should generally transform consistently under conversion from dollars to cents; GGQD does not automatically satisfy that property. Its output may exceed the input normal price, and for sufficiently large universality u it may become negative when A is positive. These are not algebraic contradictions, but they constrain a price interpretation.

Research status: Internally consistent on the photographed numerical case; mathematical properties conditionally established; economic predictive validity unverified.

VI · Falsification protocol

Define a measurable target for GGQD and an independently observable universality index. Pre-register parameter units and expected baseline identities. Evaluate numerical stability, currency scaling, extreme cases, and error propagation. Then compare out-of-sample predictions against CPI-based real-price adjustment and simpler benchmark models, reporting absolute error, squared error, bias, calibration, and negative-output frequency. Report failures alongside successes.

VII · Scholarly context

Relevant conceptual benchmarks include W. Erwin Diewert's work on index-number theory, the IMF's Consumer Price Index Manual, and U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics methodology on CPI and quality adjustment. These bodies of work provide tests and comparison methods; they do not validate the exact GGQD equation.

Conclusion

GGQD is a coherent nonlinear construction with analytically tractable universality sensitivity. Whether it constitutes a useful deflation measure remains an open empirical question. The appropriate next step is independent testing with defined units, observed prices, and reproducible benchmarks.
